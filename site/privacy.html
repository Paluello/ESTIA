Privacy policy

Applies to the ESTIA apps, the ESTIA home software and this website. Last updated 27 November 2026.

In short

The people who make ESTIA do not receive your content, your contacts, your location or any analytics. There is no ESTIA account with us, no advertising, no tracking and no analytics or crash-reporting service inside the apps. What you write, the photos you share and your messages live on your home: an ESTIA instance running on a machine in your home or your community's space, run by its administrator.

Who is responsible for your data

Each home is run by its administrator — the person or group that installed it — and they are responsible for the data their home stores. ESTIA is self-hosted software: the project does not run your home and cannot see into it. If you want to know what your home keeps, or to have your account deleted, ask its administrator.

The ESTIA project is responsible only for the few things described under "What leaves your home" that it operates itself, and for this website.

What your home stores

  • Your account: username, display name and bio, and a password hash (Argon2id). The password itself is never stored.
  • What you publish: posts, comments, likes and photos. Photos are stripped of Exif metadata, including the location where they were taken, before they are kept.
  • Your private messages, end-to-end encrypted with MLS (RFC 9420). Your home stores them in a form it cannot read; the keys are only on your devices. The other person's home keeps only a placeholder (who wrote, when) and fetches the content from your home when it is read.
  • Your devices and sessions, so you can see and revoke them, and a log of administrative actions (invites, approvals, moderation).

The administrator of a home can see everything the home stores, except the content of private messages. Backups are encrypted with a key the home itself does not hold.

What leaves your home

ESTIA homes and apps talk to each other directly, by public key. A few outside services are involved, and this is everything each of them can see. None of them can read content.

Service When What it can see
Discovery (DNS run by n0, the makers of iroh) A device or home looks up another home's key Which key looks up which key, when, and IP addresses
Relay (run by a community volunteer, or by n0 as a last resort) Only when two devices cannot connect directly That one key talks to another, when and how much, and IP addresses. Traffic is encrypted end to end and not kept. A relay run by someone near you may be able to tell whose keys those are.
Notification wake-up (optional, chosen by your home) Your home wants your phone to check for something new That a home wakes a device token, and when. The notification is empty: no sender, no text, no conversation. On iPhone it passes through Apple's push service, on Android through the service your phone uses. The project operates the forwarding service for the published app, because Apple requires the publisher's key.
App stores (Apple, Google) You download or update the app What the stores themselves collect, under their own policies
Image registry (GitHub) A home is installed or checks for updates The home's IP address and which version it downloads

Publishing to the wider Fediverse through ActivityPub is planned but not available yet. When it is, it will be off by default, and content shared that way will be copied by the servers that receive it, because ActivityPub works by copies.

What the apps keep on your device

The keys that identify your device and decrypt your messages, kept in the device's secure storage where available, and a local cache of what you have read. Uninstalling the app removes them. Your home keeps your account until you or its administrator delete it.

Permissions the apps ask for

  • Camera, to scan the QR code that introduces your home, and to take photos you choose to post.
  • Photos, only for the pictures you pick to post.
  • Notifications, if you want to be told when something new arrives.
  • Local network, to find and reach your home when you are on the same network.

The apps never ask for your contacts or your location.

Children

ESTIA is not directed at children under 13, or under the age of digital consent where you live. Whether a home admits younger members is up to its administrator and the people who share it, under the law that applies to them.

Your rights

You can see and edit your profile, delete what you published and revoke your devices from the app. For anything else your home stores about you — a copy, a correction, the deletion of your account — ask your home's administrator, who holds that data. If you are in the European Union, the GDPR gives you these rights towards them. The ESTIA project holds no personal data about you to give back or delete, apart from what is described above.

This website

This site sets no cookies, has no analytics and loads nothing from other servers. It is hosted on GitHub Pages; GitHub keeps server logs, including IP addresses, under its own privacy statement.

Changes and contact

This policy lives in ESTIA's public repository, so every change is visible with its date. Questions about it: open an issue. To report a security problem privately, follow the security policy.
