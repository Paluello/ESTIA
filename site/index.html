A social network that lives in your home.

ESTIA is a real social network — photos, posts, private chats — whose content stays on a small machine in your flat, your building or your community centre. Encrypted, with no algorithm, no ads, and no server run by the people who make it.

Five words, at the same time

  • Owned
  • Shared
  • Communal
  • Protected
  • Connected with anyone

Each of these already exists somewhere on its own. ESTIA's point is to demand all five together: your content is physically yours, your community shares a place, and you can still reach anyone in the world — without your content moving anywhere.

How it works

A home, not a platform

Each community runs a home: ESTIA on a NAS, a mini-PC or an old laptop that stays on. The database, the photos and the keys are on that machine, and nowhere else.

Three surfaces, one identity

The local feed of the people who share the home; a profile that reaches other homes; and chats that cross homes. One account, and the default is always the smallest circle.

Visited, not copied

When someone reads you from another home, they visit your content; they do not get a copy to keep. Delete a post, and it is really gone.

Found by key

Homes find each other by public key, with no domain and no open ports. When two routers will not connect directly, a community relay forwards encrypted packets it cannot read and does not keep.

Private messages are end-to-end

Chats use MLS (RFC 9420). The home that stores your messages cannot read them, and neither can its administrator.

No algorithm, no ads

Your feed is chronological and made of the people you follow and your own home. Nobody receives "all of ESTIA", and nothing becomes public by omission.

Set up a home

On a machine that stays on — a NAS, a Linux mini-PC, a Raspberry Pi — run one command. It asks the language your home will speak, installs Docker if your Linux does not have it yet (after asking), starts the home and prints the address to open from your phone. 

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Paluello/ESTIA/main/install.sh | sh

No domain, no certificate, no port forwarding. On a NAS, install Docker first from the app centre (Container Manager on Synology, Container Station on QNAP). The full guide, with backups and disk encryption, is in the installation guide.

Where the project stands

ESTIA is pre-1.0, built in the open, and says honestly what exists.

State What
Proven on real hardware Feed, comments, likes and photos; encrypted backups and restore; content crossing homes
Built, field test open End-to-end encrypted private messages between homes
Being built Apps for iPhone, Android and desktop; several devices per person; group chats across many homes; federated moderation
Not yet Store releases, push notifications, an independent security audit

The promise, stated precisely

No central application server run by the developers, and no community content kept outside the home, unless its administrator explicitly chooses so. DNS, push notification services and relays can be outside services: they are declared, and replaceable.
